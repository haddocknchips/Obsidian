## Proposed objective

> Develop and test a funder-ready investment and financing strategy for zoliflodacin access in Thailand, with a pathway to a broader Southeast Asian proposition, grounded in validated demand, pricing, cost, operating-model and impact assumptions.

## Phase 0: Alignment and evidence baseline

This should happen before a full commitment.

### Deliverables

- Agreed project objective and decision questions.
- Document and data inventory.
- Assumptions register, identifying what is validated, provisional or missing.
- Responsibility matrix covering you, Yann Ferrisse’s team, Jeff Rowland’s team and technical contributors.
- Agreed timetable and review process.
- Refined scope and deliverables.

### Required GARDP inputs

- Latest Thailand and regional forecasts.
- WTP and price-sensitivity work.
- Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories business case and relevant agreement parameters.
- Transfer-price and COGS trajectory.
- Public/private channel assumptions.
- Regulatory and market-access plan.
- Status of PMAC planning and existing partner discussions.
- Existing donor intelligence.
- Details of the introduction study and other health-economic work.
- GARDP’s available programme funding and risk appetite.
- Axmed mandate, proposal, role and due-diligence status.

## Phase 1: Integrated investment-case and financing assessment

The two Yann Ferrisse workstreams should be combined here.

### Deliverables

1. **Validated launch-economic model**
    
    - low, base and high demand;
    - pricing by channel;
    - full cost stack;
    - annual financing gap;
    - transition assumptions;
    - sensitivity analysis.
2. **Impact and additionality case**
    
    - health outcomes;
    - patient-access outcomes;
    - AMR and stewardship relevance;
    - health-system and cost-of-care impact;
    - what happens with and without external support.
3. **Mechanism assessment**
    
    - grant-supported price-gap mechanism;
    - results-linked buy-down;
    - Thailand-specific margin or price-gap support;
    - revenue floor;
    - volume guarantee;
    - concessional or blended finance;
    - COGS/process-improvement financing;
    - demand-side affordability options.
4. **Partner segmentation and prioritisation**
    
    - mandate fit;
    - instrument fit;
    - geography;
    - ticket size;
    - timeline;
    - counterparty requirement;
    - probability of engagement;
    - GARDP relationship or access;
    - recommended ask.
5. **Funder-ready investment case and concept note**
    
    - one core case;
    - modular variants for donors, DFIs and market-shaping organisations;
    - specific ask and use of proceeds;
    - governance and results framework;
    - risks and mitigations.

## Phase 2: Market testing and PMAC preparation

This is where Jeff Rowland’s team becomes especially important.

### Deliverables

- Six to ten priority counterparties, rather than a very broad catalogue.
- Engagement briefs and tailored asks.
- PMAC stakeholder list and meeting objectives.
- Talking points and concise presentation materials.
- Meeting log and feedback tracker.
- Revised investment case following market testing.
- Recommendation on whether to progress to structuring.

I would treat support for actual negotiations or transaction structuring as a **separately agreed phase**, because it could require financial, legal, tax and country-specific expertise beyond strategic advisory support.
